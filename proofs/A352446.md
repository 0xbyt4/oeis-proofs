# A352446, A341309 and A352269: parts in partitions into consecutive parts

## Statements

A partition of n into k consecutive parts is a representation

```math
n = a + (a+1) + \dots + (a+k-1), \qquad a \ge 1,\ k \ge 1 .
```

Four OEIS entries count the parts of such partitions:

- [A204217](https://oeis.org/A204217): defined by the generating function
  $\sum_{k \ge 1} k\, x^{k(k+1)/2} / (1 - x^k)$. The entry records as proved
  that A204217(n) is the total number of parts over all k.
- [A341309](https://oeis.org/A341309): defined as the sum of the odd divisors
  of n that are at most A003056(n). Since A003056(n) is the largest k with
  $k(k+1)/2 \le n$, this is the sum of the odd divisors d of n with
  $d(d+1)/2 \le n$.
- [A352446](https://oeis.org/A352446): defined as the total number of parts
  over all even k.
- [A352269](https://oeis.org/A352269): the triangle T(n, k), equal to the
  number of parts of the partition of n into $2k-1$ consecutive parts, or 0 if
  there is none, for $1 \le k \le \lfloor(\sqrt{8n+1}+1)/4\rfloor$ (the row
  length given in the formula section of that entry).

The entries contain the following statements, all marked as conjectures on
2026-11-26:

1. (A352446, 2024) The generating function of A352446 is
   $\sum_{k \ge 1} 2k\, x^{k(2k+1)} / (1 - x^{2k})$.
2. (A341309, 2022) A341309(n) is the total number of parts in all partitions
   of n into an odd number of consecutive parts.
3. (A341309, 2022, repeated without date in A352446)
   A352446(n) = A204217(n) - A341309(n).
4. (A341309 and A352269, 2022) The row sums of A352269 give A341309.

**Theorem.** All four statements are true.

Statements 1 and 2 are the two independent results. Statements 3 and 4 follow
from Statement 2.

## Prior work

Checked on 2026-11-27. The four statements are still marked as conjectures in
the OEIS. A literature search found no source that states them as proved.

The statements are weighted forms of a classical bijection. A number n has a
partition into k consecutive parts under a divisibility condition on k that
goes back to Sylvester [1]; Hirschhorn and Hirschhorn [2] give a short modern
treatment and separate odd and even k. A formula in A237048 states the
same conditions: an odd k occurs if and only if k divides n, and an even k if
and only if k divides n - k/2. A204217 records that its generating function
counts the total number of parts over all k, with a proof by Joerg Arndt
(2017) linked from that entry. A131576 gives the generating function for the
number of partitions into an even number of consecutive parts; Statement 1 is
the same series with weight 2k.

This note therefore settles conjecture labels with short arguments. It does
not claim a new method.

Related statements:

- A352425 carries the same conjecture as Statement 2. Statement 2 settles it.
- A352269 has a second conjecture, about the positions of zeros in A347529,
  A351819 and A347263. This note does not address it.

## Proof

For given $k \ge 1$ the equation above reads

```math
n = k a + \frac{k(k-1)}{2} . \tag{1}
```

For each pair (n, k) there is at most one $a \ge 1$ satisfying (1), and each
solution contributes k parts.

**Statement 1.** Let $k = 2h$ be even. Then (1) becomes

```math
n = 2h a + h(2h-1) = h(2h+1) + 2h(a-1) .
```

As a runs over the positive integers, n runs over the exponents of
$x^{h(2h+1)} / (1 - x^{2h})$, each exactly once. Each solution contributes
$2h$ parts, so

```math
\sum_{n \ge 1} A352446(n)\, x^n = \sum_{h \ge 1} \frac{2h\, x^{h(2h+1)}}{1 - x^{2h}} .
```

**Statement 2.** Let k be odd. Then $(k-1)/2$ is an integer, so (1) says that
k divides n and $a = n/k - (k-1)/2$. The condition $a \ge 1$ is equivalent to
$n \ge k(k+1)/2$. So the odd k that occur are exactly the odd divisors k of n
with $k(k+1)/2 \le n$, each contributing k parts. The total is the sum of
these divisors, which is A341309(n).

**Statement 3.** Equation (1) can be written $n = k(k+1)/2 + k(a-1)$, so the
coefficient of $x^n$ in $\sum_k k\, x^{k(k+1)/2}/(1 - x^k)$ is the total
number of parts over all k. This is the fact recorded in A204217; it is
reproved here to keep the argument self-contained. Every partition into
consecutive parts has either an even or an odd number of parts, so
A204217(n) is the sum of the totals for even k and for odd k. By the
definition of A352446 and by Statement 2 these are A352446(n) and A341309(n).

**Statement 4.** By definition T(n, k) is $2k-1$ if n has a partition into
$2k-1$ consecutive parts and 0 otherwise. By the proof of Statement 2 such a
partition exists only if $(2k-1) \cdot 2k/2 \le n$, that is
$2k - 1 \le K$ with $K = A003056(n) = \lfloor(\sqrt{8n+1}-1)/2\rfloor$. This
is equivalent to $k \le \lfloor (K+1)/2 \rfloor = \lfloor(\sqrt{8n+1}+1)/4\rfloor$,
which is the row length. So row n contains every odd number of parts that
occurs, and its sum is the total of Statement 2. $\blacksquare$

## Remark

The same computation gives a divisor formula for A352446: for $k = 2h$,
equation (1) is $n = h(2a + 2h - 1)$, so

```math
A352446(n) = \sum 2h ,
```

summed over the divisors h of n such that $n/h$ is odd and $n/h \ge 2h + 1$.

## Formalization

`OeisProofs/A352446.lean` contains a complete Lean 4 proof of Statements 1
and 2. A partition into k consecutive parts starting at a is represented as a
pair `(k, a)` with `1 <= k <= n`, `1 <= a <= n` and
`a + (a + 1) + ... + (a + k - 1) = n`; the bounds `k <= n` and `a <= n` hold
for every such partition, so nothing is lost. The definitions `evenTotal` and
`oddTotal` sum k over the pairs with k even or odd. The theorems
`evenTotal_data` and `oddTotal_data` are kernel-checked proofs that they
reproduce the first 12 terms of A352446 and A341309 listed in the OEIS.

- `oddTotal_eq` is Statement 2: `oddTotal n` equals the sum of the odd
  divisors d of n with `d * (d + 1) <= 2 * n`.
- `evenTotal_eq` is Statement 1 in coefficient form: `evenTotal n` equals the
  sum of `2 * h` over all `h >= 1` such that `n - h * (2 * h + 1)` is a
  nonnegative multiple of `2 * h`. That sum is the coefficient of `x^n` in
  the conjectured generating function. The power series itself is not
  formalized.

The Lean statement of Statement 2 uses the condition `d * (d + 1) <= 2 * n`
in place of `d <= A003056(n)`. The two conditions are equivalent by the
definition of A003056; that equivalence is not formalized.

Statements 3 and 4 are not formalized; given Statement 2 they are the short
arguments above. The proofs depend only on the standard axioms
`propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.
