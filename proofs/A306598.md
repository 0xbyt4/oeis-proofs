# A306598: sign of the circulant determinant of the divisors of n

## Statement

OEIS [A306598](https://oeis.org/A306598) defines a(n) as the determinant of the
circulant matrix whose first column lists the divisors of n in increasing
order (the title does not fix the order; the example and the programs in the
entry do). Its formula section says:

> Apparently, a(n) > 0 iff n is a square.

Status on 2026-11-26: listed as an observation without proof (the entry dates
from 2019). We prove it, in a stronger form.

**Theorem 1.** Let $t \ge 1$ and let $0 < c_0 < c_1 < \dots < c_{t-1}$ be real
numbers. Let $C$ be the $t \times t$ circulant matrix with first column
$(c_0, \dots, c_{t-1})$, that is $C_{ij} = c_{(i-j) \bmod t}$. Then
$\det C \ne 0$ and the sign of $\det C$ is $(-1)^{t-1}$.

**Corollary 2.** a(n) > 0 if n is a square and a(n) < 0 otherwise. In
particular a(n) is never 0.

## Prior work

Checked on 2026-11-27. The OEIS entry still shows the statement as
"Apparently". A literature search found no proof of the OEIS statement and no
statement of the sign rule in Theorem 1.

The nonsingularity part of Theorem 1 is known. Guo and Zhang [2]
prove that a circulant matrix is invertible if its defining sequence is real
and strictly monotone with nonzero sum. Kra and Simanca [1] survey circulant
matrices, including the product formula of Step 1. Step 2 below is therefore
not new; it is included to keep the proof self-contained. The contribution of
this note is the sign of the determinant (Step 3) and its application to
A306598.

## Proof of Theorem 1

Put $f(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} c_k x^k$ and $\omega = e^{2\pi i/t}$.

**Step 1 (eigenvalues).** For $0 \le j < t$ let $v_j$ be the vector with
entries $(v_j)_k = \omega^{jk}$. Then

```math
(C v_j)_i = \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} c_{(i-k) \bmod t}\,\omega^{jk}
          = \sum_{m=0}^{t-1} c_m\,\omega^{j(i-m)}
          = f(\omega^{-j})\,(v_j)_i .
```

The vectors $v_0, \dots, v_{t-1}$ form a Vandermonde matrix with distinct
nodes, so they are a basis of eigenvectors and

```math
\det C = \prod_{j=0}^{t-1} f(\omega^{-j}) = \prod_{j=0}^{t-1} f(\omega^{j}).
```

**Step 2 (no zero on the unit circle).** We claim $f(z) \ne 0$ whenever
$|z| = 1$.

If $z = 1$ then $f(1) = \sum_k c_k > 0$. This also settles $t = 1$. Now let
$t \ge 2$, $|z| = 1$, $z \ne 1$, and suppose $f(z) = 0$. Expanding
$(1-z) f(z) = 0$ gives

```math
c_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{t-1} (c_k - c_{k-1})\, z^k = c_{t-1}\, z^t .
```

Every coefficient on the left is positive and the coefficients sum to
$c_{t-1}$. Taking absolute values, the left side has modulus $c_{t-1}$, which
is the sum of the moduli of its terms. Equality in the triangle inequality
forces all terms to have the same argument. The term $c_0$ is a positive real,
so $(c_1 - c_0)\, z$ is a positive real, hence $z = 1$, a contradiction.

**Step 3 (sign).** The coefficients of $f$ are real, so
$f(\omega^{t-j}) = \overline{f(\omega^{j})}$. Group the factors of
$\det C$:

- $j = 0$ contributes $f(1) > 0$.
- Each pair $\{j, t-j\}$ with $0 < j < t/2$ contributes
  $|f(\omega^j)|^2$, which is positive by Step 2.
- If $t$ is even, $j = t/2$ contributes

```math
f(-1) = \sum_{l=0}^{t/2-1} (c_{2l} - c_{2l+1}) < 0 .
```

So $\det C > 0$ when $t$ is odd and $\det C < 0$ when $t$ is even. $\blacksquare$

## Proof of Corollary 2

Apply Theorem 1 to the divisors $d_0 < d_1 < \dots < d_{t-1}$ of n, where
$t = \tau(n)$. The map $d \mapsto n/d$ pairs the divisors of n, with a fixed
point exactly when n is a square, so $\tau(n)$ is odd if and only if n is a
square. $\blacksquare$

## Remarks

1. The proof gives the factorization

   ```math
   a(n) = \sigma(n) \cdot \prod_{j=1}^{\tau(n)-1} f(\omega^j),
   \qquad f(x) = \sum_k d_k x^k .
   ```

   After pairing complex conjugate factors, the only negative factor is
   $f(-1)$. It occurs for non-squares and equals $-A071324(n)$, where
   A071324 is the alternating sum of the divisors starting with n. The
   product of the remaining factors is an integer: if g is a monic polynomial
   with integer coefficients, the product of $f$ over the roots of g is the
   resultant $\mathrm{Res}(g, f)$. Here $g = (x^t-1)/(x-1)$ for squares and
   $g = (x^t-1)/(x^2-1)$ for non-squares, with $t = \tau(n)$. Hence
   $\sigma(n)$ divides a(n) for all n, and $\sigma(n) \cdot A071324(n)$
   divides a(n) for non-squares. This proves the two divisibility comments in
   the OEIS entry.
2. Step 2 is a strict form of the Enestrom-Kakeya theorem. The classical
   theorem, for weakly increasing positive coefficients, only places the
   zeros in the closed unit disk ($1 + x$ has a zero on the circle). Strict
   monotonicity excludes the circle. The short argument is included to keep
   the proof self-contained.
3. Nothing in the proof uses divisors. Any strictly increasing positive
   sequence gives a circulant determinant of sign $(-1)^{t-1}$.
4. On 2026-11-27 the formula section of the entry also said
   "a(p) = p^2 - 1 for any prime number p". The sign is wrong. The divisors
   of p are 1 and p, so $a(p) = 1 - p^2$, in agreement with Corollary 2 and
   with the data $a(2) = -3$, $a(3) = -8$.

## Formalization

`OeisProofs/A306598.lean` contains a complete Lean 4 proof. The sequence is
defined as

```lean
def divisorList (n : Nat) : List Nat := n.divisors.sort (fun x y => x <= y)

def a (n : Nat) : Int :=
  (circulant fun i : Fin (divisorList n).length => ((divisorList n)[i] : Int)).det
```

(written here with ASCII spellings), where Mathlib's `Matrix.circulant v` is
the matrix with entries `v (i - j)`, so its first column is `v`. The
definition is computable and evaluates to the OEIS data. The main theorems
are

- `a_pos_iff : 0 < a n <-> IsSquare n` (the conjecture),
- `a_neg_of_not_isSquare` and `a_ne_zero` (the stronger form of Corollary 2),
- `det_circulant_pos_of_odd` and `det_circulant_neg_of_even` (Theorem 1, for
  real strictly increasing positive vectors indexed by `Fin t`).

Steps 1 and 2 are formalized as written: `det_circulant_eq_prod` is the
product formula and `sum_mul_pow_ne_zero` is the statement that there is no
zero on the unit circle. Step 3 is replaced by a continuity argument: along
the segment from c to the last basis vector the determinant never vanishes,
so its sign equals the sign at the end point, a cyclic permutation matrix
with determinant `(-1)^(t-1)`.

The two divisibility statements of Remark 1 are also formalized:

- `sigma_dvd_a`: if `n != 0`, then `(sigma 1 n : Int)` divides `a n`,
- `sigma_mul_alternatingDivisorSum_dvd_a`: if `n` is not a square, then
  `(sigma 1 n : Int) * alternatingDivisorSum n` divides `a n`.

Here `sigma 1 n` is Mathlib's `ArithmeticFunction.sigma 1 n`, the sum of the
divisors of n. `alternatingDivisorSum n` is A071324(n), defined as the
`List.alternatingSum` of the divisors of n in decreasing order. The formal
proofs do not use resultants. They use two general lemmas about circulant
matrices over a commutative ring:

- `sum_dvd_det_circulant`: for size t >= 1, the sum of the entries of the
  first column divides the determinant. The all-ones vector is an
  eigenvector, and the proof multiplies by the adjugate.
- `sum_mul_alternating_sum_dvd_det_circulant`: for even size t >= 2,
  f(1) f(-1) divides the determinant. An integer change of basis with
  determinant 1 keeps the vectors (1, 0, 1, 0, ...) and (0, 1, 0, 1, ...).
  It makes the matrix block triangular with the 2 x 2 block
  [[E, O], [O, E]], where E and O are the sums of the entries with even and
  odd index. The determinant of this block is (E + O)(E - O) = f(1) f(-1).

The proofs depend only on the standard axioms `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.
