# A365849: all terms after the first are congruent to 1 modulo 36

## Statement

OEIS [A365849](https://oeis.org/A365849) lists the triangular numbers that
are the product of two distinct primes of the form 4k + 3. A comment in the
entry says:

> Apparently, a(n) == 1 (mod 36) for n >= 2.

Status on 2026-11-26: listed as an observation without proof (since 2023).

**Theorem.**

1. The terms of A365849 are exactly the numbers $r(2r+1)$ where r and
   $2r+1$ are both prime and $r \equiv 3 \pmod 4$.
2. The first term is 21 (from r = 3). Every other term comes from a prime
   $r \equiv 11 \pmod{12}$ and is congruent to 1 modulo 36.
3. The modulus 36 is optimal: if all terms after the first are congruent to
   each other modulo M, then M divides 36.

## Prior work

Checked on 2026-11-27. The OEIS entry still shows the statement as
"Apparently". A literature search found no proof.

The ingredients are known. A triangular number that is a product of two
primes has the form $p(2p \pm 1)$ (A068443). The primes $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$
with $2p+1$ prime are the Lucasian primes (A002515), a subset of the Sophie
Germain primes (A005384). The theorem below combines these facts; the
argument is short and elementary.

## Proof

**Part 1.** Let $T = m(m+1)/2$ with $m \ge 1$ and suppose $T = pq$ with
distinct primes p, q, both congruent to 3 modulo 4. Write $T = uv$ with

- $u = m/2$, $v = m+1$ if m is even,
- $u = m$, $v = (m+1)/2$ if m is odd.

In both cases $\gcd(u, v) = 1$, since $\gcd(m, m+1) = 1$. A factorization of
$pq$ into two coprime factors is $1 \cdot pq$ or $p \cdot q$. If $u = 1$ or
$v = 1$ then $m \le 2$ and $T \in \{1, 3\}$, which is not a product of two
primes. Hence $\{u, v\} = \{p, q\}$.

- If m is odd, put $r = (m+1)/2$. The primes are r and $2r - 1$. Both
  primes must be congruent to 3 modulo 4. But $r \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ gives
  $2r - 1 \equiv 1 \pmod 4$. So m is not odd.
- If m is even, put $r = m/2$. The primes are r and $2r + 1$, and
  $r \equiv 3 \pmod 4$.

So $T = r(2r+1)$ with r and $2r+1$ prime and $r \equiv 3 \pmod 4$.
Conversely, for such r the number $r(2r+1)$ is the triangular number with
$m = 2r$, and $2r + 1 \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ holds automatically.

**Part 2.** For r = 3 we get $3 \cdot 7 = 21$. Let $r > 3$. Then
$3 \nmid r$. If $r \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ then $3 \mid 2r+1$ and $2r + 1 > 3$, so
$2r+1$ is not prime. Hence $r \equiv 2 \pmod 3$. Together with
$r \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ this gives $r \equiv 11 \pmod{12}$. Write
$r = 12s + 11$ with $s \ge 0$. Then

```math
r(2r+1) = (12s+11)(24s+23) = 288s^2 + 540s + 253 = 36\,(8s^2 + 15s + 7) + 1 .
```

**Part 3.** The numbers 253, 1081 and 13861 are terms (r = 11, 23, 83). Their
differences are $1081 - 253 = 36 \cdot 23$ and $13861 - 253 = 36 \cdot 378$,
and $\gcd(23, 378) = 1$. A modulus M for which these three terms are
congruent to each other divides both differences, hence divides 36.
$\blacksquare$

## Formalization

`OeisProofs/A365849.lean` contains a complete Lean 4 proof of all three
parts. The statements below use ASCII spellings of the Lean file.

The sequence is the predicate `IsTerm`:

```lean
def IsTerm (t : Nat) : Prop :=
  (exists m, m * (m + 1) = 2 * t) /\
    exists p q, p.Prime /\ q.Prime /\ Ne p q /\ p % 4 = 3 /\ q % 4 = 3 /\ t = p * q
```

The first conjunct says that t is the triangular number `m (m + 1) / 2`.
The second conjunct says that t is the product of two distinct primes of the
form 4k + 3.

Part 1 is `isTerm_iff`:

```lean
theorem isTerm_iff {t : Nat} :
    IsTerm t <-> exists r, r.Prime /\ r % 4 = 3 /\ (2 * r + 1).Prime /\
      t = r * (2 * r + 1)
```

Part 2 is `isTerm_mod_36`. The file also proves that 21 is a term
(`isTerm_21`) and that every term is at least 21 (`le_of_isTerm`):

```lean
theorem isTerm_mod_36 {t : Nat} (ht : IsTerm t) (h21 : Ne t 21) : t % 36 = 1
theorem isTerm_21 : IsTerm 21
theorem le_of_isTerm {t : Nat} (ht : IsTerm t) : 21 <= t
```

The theorem `modEq_one` states the same congruence directly in terms of
m, p and q, and `isTerm_mod_36` uses it.

Part 3 uses the terms 253, 1081 and 13861 (`isTerm_253`, `isTerm_1081`,
`isTerm_13861`). The optimality statement is `dvd_36_of_forall_modEq`:

```lean
theorem dvd_36_of_forall_modEq {M : Nat}
    (h : forall s t, IsTerm s -> IsTerm t -> Ne s 21 -> Ne t 21 ->
      s % M = t % M) :
    Dvd.dvd M 36
```

`forall_modEq_iff_dvd_36` combines parts 2 and 3. It states that all terms
other than 21 have the same residue modulo M if and only if M divides 36.

The proofs depend only on the standard axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`
and `Quot.sound`.
